ProbToE

Probabilistic Tree of Embeddings for uncertainty-aware long-horizon video reasoning.

Abstract

Processing raw video sequences for long-horizon tasks is computationally intractable due to massive temporal redundancy and high dimensionality. Consequently, high-fidelity reasoning and planning in long-form video understanding require compact, abstracted representations of the internal world state. Current state-of-the-art frameworks address this by constructing a deterministic “tree of captions”, which compresses hierarchical video segments into textual descriptions. However, this early captioning approach introduces a severe information bottleneck; it is inherently lossy, discards critical visual nuances, and fails to capture the inherent semantic ambiguity of the underlying video data.

To resolve this, we propose a probabilistic tree of embeddings, which models hierarchical video segments as multivariate Gaussian distributions directly in the embedding space. This preserves visual uncertainty and delays decoding until task-specific reasoning is required. While effective, estimating these distributions can be computationally heavy. To eliminate this bottleneck, we introduce an amortized inference adapter trained via Conditional Flow Matching. Through self-supervised learning on raw video sequences, the adapter learns the continuous vector fields of frame dispersion to capture true aleatoric uncertainty. At inference, this lightweight network instantly maps a deterministic video chunk context into fully parameterized Gaussian distributions, entirely bypassing stochastic sampling. This approach provides a mathematically rigorous, highly scalable foundation for uncertainty-aware world models capable of complex, long-horizon video reasoning.
